Interior Styling Tips Before an Airbnb Photoshoot

Styling for property photography is about clarity, not over-decoration. The aim is to make the space feel clean, welcoming and easy to understand while keeping the guest’s attention on the property itself.

Good styling for an Airbnb photoshoot is restrained. The goal is not to make the property look like a showroom. The goal is to make it feel clean, usable, calm and guest-ready, while keeping attention on the space itself.

The most common mistake is adding more: more cushions, more flowers, more trays, more candles, more signs. In photographs, small items accumulate quickly. Strong styling usually starts by taking things away, then adding back only what supports the guest experience.

Styling principle

Every visible item should pass one test: does it make the property feel clearer, cleaner, more comfortable or more useful to a guest? If not, it is probably visual noise.

Start by removing visual noise

Clear kitchen worktops, bedside tables, bathroom shelves, coffee tables and entrance areas. Hide bins, cables, remote controls, cleaning products, paperwork, excess ornaments and anything too personal. Then look again. Most rooms need less than hosts expect.

Neutral does not have to mean empty. A room can still feel warm with simple textiles, good bedding, a plant, natural light and one or two well-placed details. The difference is that the details support the room rather than compete with it.

Style for the camera, not just the room

A room that feels fine in person may still look messy from the camera angle. Objects near the edge of the frame can feel accidental. Chairs may need small adjustments. Cushions that look casual in person may look collapsed in a still image. Beds often need more tension and symmetry than they do during a normal changeover.

Make beds and sofas camera-ready

Beds and sofas dominate many room photographs. They need to look deliberate. Pull duvets tight, align pillows, remove uneven cushions, check valances or throws, and step back to view the result from the doorway. What looks small up close often becomes obvious in a photograph.

Sofas should look usable, not decorative only. Avoid filling every seat with cushions. Guests want to understand comfort and capacity. A balanced arrangement usually photographs better than a pile of styling items.

Styling details that photograph well

  • Fresh bedding, neatly folded towels and simple cushions.
  • Natural textures such as wood, linen, plants or ceramics.
  • One or two local touches, such as a guidebook, map or coastal detail.
  • A clean coffee or breakfast setup where it feels believable.
  • Outdoor seating arranged as if guests could use it immediately.

Use colour deliberately

Colour should help the room feel coherent. A small number of repeated tones usually works better than many unrelated accents. If the room already has a strong feature wall, patterned tiles or colourful furniture, keep accessories simpler so the image does not become busy.

Also watch for colour temperature. Mixed bulbs can make one area look yellow and another look blue. Replacing mismatched bulbs before a shoot can make editing cleaner and the final gallery more consistent.

What to avoid before the photographer arrives

Over-personalising

Family photos, paperwork, personal toiletries and private items make the space feel less guest-ready.

Over-staging

Champagne glasses, excessive candles or fake breakfast scenes can look forced unless they genuinely fit the property.

Ignoring function

A property should look usable: enough seating, clear worktops, accessible beds and practical outdoor areas.

Leaving tired details

Flat cushions, marked towels, dead plants and worn bath mats read poorly in still images.
