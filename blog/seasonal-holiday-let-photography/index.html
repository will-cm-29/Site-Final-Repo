Seasonal Holiday Let Photography: When to Refresh Your Listing Images

Holiday-let photography should stay aligned with the guest experience. If a property changes, or if seasonal appeal is a major part of the booking decision, refreshed photography can help the listing feel current and accurate.

Holiday-let photography should keep pace with the real guest experience. If the property has changed, the garden looks different, the furniture has been upgraded or the target season has shifted, old images can quietly hold the listing back.

A refresh does not always mean replacing the whole gallery. Often the strongest approach is to keep the core room set and add a smaller group of updated images: summer garden, winter living room, new hot tub, upgraded bedding, renovated kitchen, improved parking or refreshed exterior.

Keep the listing aligned with the real stay

Seasonal updates matter because guests rely on images to set expectations. If the photos no longer match the property, the listing can create avoidable disappointment even when the accommodation is still good.

Refresh images after meaningful changes

New furniture, repainted rooms, upgraded bedding, garden improvements, new outdoor seating, EV charging, hot tubs, workspaces and kitchen changes should all be photographed. If you have invested in the property, the images should show that investment clearly.

Do not assume guests will notice updates in the written description. Many will skim. A new amenity only helps conversion if it is visible, credible and placed in the gallery where guests can find it.

Seasonality changes what guests care about

Summer guests may care about gardens, outdoor dining, beaches, light, parking and group space. Winter guests may care more about heating, cosy living areas, workspaces, baths, fireplaces, reading corners and comfortable bedrooms. The same property can need a different visual emphasis at different times of year.

Accuracy matters for reviews

Outdated images can create avoidable disappointment. If a sofa, garden, bed setup, decor scheme or amenity has changed, guests may feel misled even if the current version is better. Accurate images protect expectations and reduce friction.

This is particularly important for outdoor images. Gardens, planting, views and weather can change the feel of a property. Do not keep a peak-summer garden image as the main selling point if the property looks very different for most of the booking season.

Refresh your photos when:

  • You redecorate, replace furniture or change bedroom layouts.
  • You add a major amenity such as hot tub, EV charger, workspace or outdoor dining.
  • The garden, exterior or view becomes a stronger selling point.
  • You want to target a different season or guest type.
  • The current gallery no longer feels like the property guests will arrive to.

Small refresh or full reshoot?

A small refresh works when the core gallery is still strong and only a few areas have changed. A full reshoot is better when the edit looks inconsistent, the hero image is weak, multiple rooms have changed, or the original gallery no longer matches the price position.

Do not keep adding new images from different seasons, devices and editing styles without reviewing the whole set. A patched gallery can feel less professional than a shorter, coherent one.
