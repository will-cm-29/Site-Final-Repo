Seasonal Holiday Let Photography: When to Refresh Your Listing Images

Holiday-let photography should stay aligned with the guest experience. If a property changes, or if seasonal appeal is a major part of the booking decision, refreshed photography can help the listing feel current and accurate.

Holiday-let photography should keep pace with the real guest experience. If the property has changed, the garden looks different, the furniture has been upgraded or the target season has shifted, old images can quietly hold the listing back.

Why the images matter commercially

Airbnb’s own Professional Photography page says listings using the service saw a 19% net uplift in bookings on average over the following 365 days, compared with similar listings without it. A separate academic Airbnb study found verified photos were associated with listings being booked 9% more frequently, with high photo quality linked to an estimated $2,455 in extra yearly earnings. Those numbers are not a promise, but they do show why photography should be treated as part of the booking strategy rather than a finishing touch.

Refresh images after meaningful changes

New furniture, a repainted room, upgraded bedding, a new kitchen, an improved garden or a better outdoor setup should all be photographed. If you have invested in the property, the listing should show that investment clearly.

Seasonality changes what guests care about

Summer guests may care about outside space, beaches, light and dining areas. Winter guests may care more about comfort, warmth, workspaces and cosy interiors. A small seasonal refresh can help the listing feel relevant to the next type of booking you want.

Accuracy matters for reviews

Outdated images can create avoidable disappointment. If a room looks different on arrival, guests may feel misled even if the property is still good. Refreshing images helps align expectations with reality, which supports better reviews.

Refresh your photos when:

  • You redecorate or change furniture
  • You add a major amenity
  • The garden or exterior becomes a selling point
  • You want to target a new season or guest type
  • The current gallery no longer feels like the property
