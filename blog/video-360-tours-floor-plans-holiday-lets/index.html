Video, 360 Tours and Floor Plans for Holiday Lets: Are They Worth It?

Still photography is usually the foundation of a holiday-let listing, but video, 360 tours and floor plans can add extra clarity. They are especially useful when layout, scale or experience is difficult to explain through photographs alone.

Still photography is usually the foundation of a holiday-let listing, but video, 360 tours and floor plans can add useful context. They are most valuable when the property has a layout, scale, setting or guest experience that still images do not fully explain.

The mistake is adding every format because it sounds impressive. Video, 360 tours and floor plans solve different problems. Video creates atmosphere. A 360 tour builds confidence. A floor plan explains layout. Drone footage explains setting. None of them should be used as a substitute for a strong still gallery.

Use video for atmosphere

Video works best when movement adds something: walking through an open-plan living space, showing how a kitchen connects to dining, moving from inside to a garden, panning across a view, or creating short clips for social media and direct booking pages.

For direct booking websites, video can make the stay feel more tangible. A calm walkthrough can help a guest understand flow, while shorter vertical edits can support Instagram, TikTok, reels, email campaigns or paid social ads. The key is restraint. A shaky, fast walkthrough can make a good property feel worse.

Use 360 tours for confidence

A 360 tour lets guests explore more actively. It can be useful for larger homes, premium properties, serviced accommodation, unusual layouts, accessible stays, wedding accommodation, group bookings or longer visits where layout questions may slow enquiries.

However, 360 tours are not always necessary. For a simple one-bedroom apartment, a clear still gallery may be quicker and more effective. The question is not whether the technology is impressive; it is whether the guest needs it to feel confident.

Use floor plans to explain the layout

Floor plans are especially useful for families, groups and longer stays. They help guests understand bedroom positions, bathrooms, stairs, access, shared spaces and whether the property works for their group. This is information that photographs can imply, but not always prove.

A floor plan can reduce uncertainty for guests travelling with children, older relatives or mixed groups. It answers questions about bedroom separation, bathroom access, stairs and communal space without forcing the guest to decode the image gallery.

The best mix for most hosts

  • Still images for the main listing gallery and first impression.
  • Video for atmosphere, direct booking pages and social media.
  • 360 tours for larger, higher-value, unusual or confidence-sensitive properties.
  • Floor plans where layout questions are common.
  • Drone images where setting, access or outdoor space adds real value.

Do not replace still photography

Guests still need a clean set of still images to compare rooms quickly. Video and 360 tours should support the gallery, not replace it. Most guests will scan still images first, then use extra formats when they are already interested.

Think of the extra formats as confidence tools. They can help a guest move from “this looks good” to “this works for our group”. They are weaker if the still photography is incomplete or inconsistent.

When the extras are probably worth it

Large layouts

Multiple bedrooms, floors, bathrooms or entrances make floor plans and 360 tours more useful.

Premium rates

Higher booking values justify more reassurance before the guest commits.

Direct bookings

Your own website benefits from extra trust signals and richer media.

Strong setting

Drone and video help when location, outdoor space or view is part of the reason to book.
