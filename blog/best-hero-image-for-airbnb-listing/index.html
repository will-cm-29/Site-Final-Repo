How to Choose the Best Hero Image for Your Airbnb Listing

The hero image is the photograph that earns the click. It appears before the guest has read the full description, checked the amenities or studied the reviews, so it needs to communicate the strongest reason to look closer.

The hero image is the first commercial decision in your Airbnb gallery. It decides whether a guest pauses on your listing or scrolls past it. A good hero image is not simply the nicest photograph from the shoot; it is the image that explains the stay fastest.

Hosts often choose emotionally: a favourite bedroom, a decorative corner, a close-up of a coffee tray, or a view they personally like. Guests choose more practically. They want to understand the property, the quality and the reason to click within a second or two.

The thumbnail test

A hero image needs to work at small mobile size, after platform cropping, among competing listings. If it only works full-screen, it is probably not the best lead image.

Choose the image that best explains the stay

Start by identifying the property’s strongest booking argument. It may be space, view, garden, interior finish, location, family practicality, coastal access or group capacity. The hero image should make that advantage visible without needing the title or description.

This is why the “best” image varies by property. A garden may outperform a bedroom for a family summer let. A living room with a sea view may outperform a kitchen for a coastal apartment. A large dining space may outperform an exterior for group accommodation.

Do not automatically lead with the bedroom

Bedrooms can work well for boutique stays, romantic breaks or unusually strong interiors. But for many holiday lets, a bedroom does not explain enough. It may not show scale, location, shared space or the reason a group should choose the property.

As a rule, lead with the image that answers: “Why this property, not another one?” If the bedroom is the answer, use it. If the property is really about the open-plan living area, outdoor dining, sea view or large kitchen, lead with that instead.

Test whether the image works small

Before uploading, view the image as a small square or landscape thumbnail on your phone. If the subject becomes unclear, if the room looks cramped, or if important detail sits too close to the edge, choose another frame or crop differently.

Strong hero images usually have simple shapes, visible light, clear depth and one obvious subject. Busy shelves, too many chairs, dark corners or heavy foreground objects often make the image harder to read in search results.

Hero image checks

  • Can the image be understood in less than two seconds?
  • Does it show the strongest reason to choose the property?
  • Does it survive mobile cropping without losing the subject?
  • Is it bright and clear without looking unrealistic?
  • Does it set an honest expectation for the rest of the gallery?

What to avoid as the first image

Avoid close-up detail shots, narrow corners, dark bedrooms, empty corridors, bathrooms unless they are exceptional, and drone images that make the actual accommodation hard to identify. These can still be useful later in the gallery, but they rarely deserve the first slot.

Also avoid choosing an image purely because it is unusual. The first image should be interesting, but clarity beats novelty. Guests should not have to work out what they are looking at.

Review performance, then adjust

The hero image is not permanent. If the listing is getting impressions but weak engagement, test another lead image. Try the living space instead of the exterior, the garden instead of the bedroom, or a brighter crop of the same room. A small change can alter how the listing competes visually.
