The hero image is the photograph that earns the click. It appears before the guest has read the full description, checked the amenities or studied the reviews, so it needs to communicate the strongest reason to look closer.

Lead with the property’s strongest appeal

The best hero image depends on the property. A stylish apartment might lead with the living space, a coastal home might lead with a balcony or view, and a family house might lead with an open-plan kitchen or garden. Choose the image that gives guests the clearest reason to click.

Check how it works as a small thumbnail

Guests often first see your listing in a grid on a phone. A good hero image should still make sense when small. Avoid images where the subject is unclear, too dark or dependent on tiny details.

Avoid misleading angles

A hero image should be attractive but honest. Overly distorted angles or images that hide obvious limitations can create disappointment later. Good photography presents the property at its best while still setting accurate expectations.

Use light, composition and simplicity

Strong hero images usually have clean lines, balanced brightness and an obvious focal point. Too much clutter makes the image harder to read. A simpler, well-composed shot often performs better visually than a busy image with many competing elements.

Refresh the hero image when the season changes

Outdoor spaces, gardens and coastal views can look very different throughout the year. If the lead image no longer reflects the current guest experience, consider refreshing it or changing the gallery order.
